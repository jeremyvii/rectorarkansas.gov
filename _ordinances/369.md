**An ordinance establishing the Master Street Plan for the City of Rector and its planning jurisdiction: Creating street classifications, standards and for other purposes**

Be it ordained by the City Council of the City of Rector, Arkansas

## Section 1.

The design consideration of the functionally classified street system of the City of Rector shall be as follows:

### A. Residential and Minor Residential Streets:

The primary function of a residential or local street is the provision of access to adjacent property, and the secondary function is the movement of traffic. This functional hierarchy plainly discourages through traffic. Buses and heavy trucks should be excluded when the abutting land use is residential. Other uses of local streets are as easements for utilities and as open space to provide air and light.

#### Design Considerations for Residential Streets

| Consideration | Standard |
| --- | --- |
| Service Volume | 2,800 vehicles per day |
| Speed | 20 miles per hour |
| Traffic Lanes | 2–10 foot through lanes |
| Paved Width | 20–24 Feet |
| Right-of-Way | 50 foot minimum |
| Sidewalk | One side |
| Maximum Grade at Centerline | 15% |
| Minimum Sight Distance at crest of Vertical Curve | 150 Feet |
| Minimum Horizontal Radius | 150 Feet |
| Minimum Horizontal Tangent Distance between Reverse Curves | N/A |

| Minimum Sight Distance at crest of Vertical Curve | 150 Feet |
| Minimum Horizontal Radius at Centerline | 75 Feet |
| Minimum Horizontal Tangent Distance between Reverse Curves | N/A |

### B. Collector Streets:

The primary and secondary functions of a collector street are the opposite of the functions of a residential street. The primary function of a collector is to conduct traffic from residential streets to arterials or to activity centers. The secondary function is the provision of access to abutting property. The collector street system should not form a continuous system, but should direct traffic to arterials. This will discourage traffic from using the collectors as arterials. The spacing of the collector system is dependent upon the density of residential neighborhoods, the use of mass transportation, and the location of community shopping centers and other large traffic generators.

#### Design Considerations

| Consideration | Standard |
| --- | --- |

## Section 2. Existing Transportation Facilities:

As a part of this ordinance a map of the existing streets in the corporate limits of the City of Rector is included and indicates the use designation. While unique conditions may prevent full compliance for these facilities, it is the intent of this ordinance to provide a guide for future improvements.

## Section 3. New Transportation Facilities:

Facilities shown, noted and designated with the Master Street Plan which are now physically existing are general locations only. At the time of development of areas which include the facility as shown, the City Council will consider the best location in regard to topography and economics.

## Section 4. Acceptance of Streets:

The City will be guided by the standards contained herein when accepting streets from private developer for dedication as public facilities.

The City Council may determine the location of additional collector streets which are not included within the Master Street Plan, but which are considered necessary for the continued development of an efficient and complete circulation system.

## Section 5.

If conflicts between this ordinance and other city ordinances should occur, the most restrictive ordinance shall prevail.

## Section 6.

This Ordinance being necessary to promote planning in the City of Rector and being further necessary for the preservation of the public place, health and safety, an emergency is hereby declared to exist and this Ordinance shall be in effect from and after its passage.
